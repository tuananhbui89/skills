# Lil'Log-Inspired Review Principles

Use these as transferable writing principles, not as a request to impersonate or reproduce an author's exact style.

## Core rubric

1. **Start with a compression model.** Give readers a small, distinct set of concepts that organizes the rest of the article. A reader should be able to reuse the model after reading.
2. **Make the scope explicit.** Define the question, audience, assumptions, and exclusions before detail accumulates.
3. **Teach in layers.** Move from a plain-language concept to its mechanism, supporting evidence, and limitation. Introduce vocabulary before applying it in a case study.
4. **Use evidence proportionately.** Link claims to primary papers, official documentation, original experiments, or first-party data. Distinguish observation, inference, and opinion.
5. **Include trade-offs.** State failure modes, operating limits, and when a simpler alternative is preferable. Do not end in triumphalism.
6. **Use case studies to recompose the system.** After readers understand components, show a concrete system that combines them and call out the mapping.
7. **Use visual teaching aids.** A figure must answer a question created by the prose: whole-system orientation, process sequence, architecture, comparison, data result, or concrete trace.
8. **Keep navigation honest.** A detailed table of contents, descriptive headings, and a stable section rhythm help readers skim a long synthesis without hiding its depth.

## Diagnostic prompts

- Can the reader state the article's central model in one sentence?
- Does every major section either introduce a necessary concept, explain a mechanism, provide evidence, show a case, or qualify a claim?
- Is a case study introduced only after its vocabulary has been taught?
- Are diagrams placed after the reader needs the question and before prose overloads working memory?
- Does every chart identify what is compared, under which conditions, and what cannot be concluded?
- Is the conclusion a usable boundary or decision rule rather than a summary of hype?

## Illustration quality bar

Use one visual for one learning job. Draft the takeaway caption first. Make the primary relationship visible in seconds through hierarchy, labels, direction, and a focus cue. Use colour redundantly with shape, labels, or line style. Split overloaded diagrams; do not shrink the lesson. Place a short explanatory caption and complete alt text with the asset.
